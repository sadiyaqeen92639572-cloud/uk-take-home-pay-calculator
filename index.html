Formula

— Income Tax (marginal bands) —
taxable = gross_salary − pension_contribution
income_tax = Σ over each band: min(taxable, band_top) − band_bottom × band_rate

— National Insurance —
ni = (min(gross,UEL)−PT) × 8% + max(0,gross−UEL) × 2%

— Student Loan —
repayment = max(0, gross − plan_threshold) × plan_rate

— Take-Home —
take_home = gross − income_tax − ni − repayment − pension_contribution

Pension modelled as a net-pay-arrangement deduction (reduces taxable income, not NI). Not a substitute for a payslip — verify against HMRC's own estimator at gov.uk/estimate-income-tax.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is UK take-home pay calculated?
Take-home pay = gross salary minus Income Tax, minus employee National Insurance, minus student loan repayments (if applicable), minus pension contributions. Income Tax uses the Personal Allowance (£12,570 for 2026/27) then 20%/40%/45% bands (different bands apply in Scotland). Employee NI is 8% between £12,570 and £50,270, then 2% above.
What is the 2026/27 Personal Allowance?
£12,570 — frozen since 2022 and due to stay frozen until 2031. It tapers down by £1 for every £2 earned above £100,000, and is fully withdrawn at £125,140.
Why is my Scotland take-home pay different from England?
Scotland sets its own Income Tax rates and bands under devolved powers — six bands (19%/20%/21%/42%/45%/48%) instead of the rest-of-UK's three (20%/40%/45%). National Insurance, student loan and pension rules are the same across the whole UK.
Does this calculator include student loan repayments?
Yes — select your Plan (1, 2, 4, 5 or Postgraduate) and the calculator deducts 9% (6% for Postgraduate) of income above the relevant annual threshold.
Is pension contribution included in the calculation?
Yes — enter your pension contribution as a percentage of salary. The calculator treats it as reducing your taxable income (net pay arrangement), which is the most common workplace pension setup.
