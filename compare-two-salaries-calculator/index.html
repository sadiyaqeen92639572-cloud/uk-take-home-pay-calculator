Salary Comparison: Gross vs Take-Home

A pay comparison on gross salary alone can mislead. Two jobs advertising the same headline number can leave different amounts in the bank once pension scheme, student loan plan, region and marginal tax rate are counted. Entering both salaries above gives a like-for-like take-home comparison instead of a gross-pay one.

Why Compare Take-Home Pay, Not Just Gross Salary

A £5,000 gross pay rise rarely means £5,000 more in your pocket — moving into a higher tax band, losing Personal Allowance above £100,000, or a different region's Income Tax bands (Scotland vs the rest of the UK) can all shrink the real-world gain. Comparing two offers on take-home pay, not headline salary, shows what actually changes for your budget.

What to Check Beyond the Numbers

  • Pension match: a lower salary with a stronger employer pension match can still be worth more overall.
  • Benefits in kind: private healthcare, company car or bonus schemes aren't captured here — factor them in separately.
  • Location: if one offer is in Scotland, its Income Tax bands differ from the rest of the UK even at an identical salary.

Get Independent Financial Advice

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my pay rise not worth as much as I expected?
Because Income Tax and National Insurance are banded, part of a pay rise can be taxed at a higher marginal rate than your existing salary — and above £100,000 you also start losing Personal Allowance at 60% effective marginal rate. Comparing take-home pay (not gross) shows the real gain.
How do I compare a Scotland-based job offer to an England-based one?
Enter each offer with its correct region — Scotland uses six Income Tax bands (19–48%) instead of the rest of the UK's three (20/40/45%), so identical gross salaries can produce different take-home pay depending on region.
Does this include pension and student loan in the comparison?
Yes — both are optional per-offer inputs, since employer pension schemes and student loan status vary between jobs and materially affect the real take-home difference.
What should I check beyond take-home pay when comparing offers?
Employer pension contribution match, private healthcare or other benefits in kind, bonus structure, and cost of commuting or relocating — none of which are captured by a salary-only comparison.
How do I compare two salaries or wages in the UK?
Enter each salary with its region, pension percentage and student loan plan above. The calculator returns take-home pay per year and per month for both, plus the difference, so you compare wages on a net basis rather than gross.
